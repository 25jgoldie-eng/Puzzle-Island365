The Recall

The facility wasn’t supposed to look like this.

Concrete walls. Fluorescent lights. A faint hum of machines buried somewhere behind the panels. Everything felt normal — painfully normal — except for the way the air vibrated, like the room itself was holding its breath.

You stood in the observation chamber, staring at the monitor.

A single red dot pulsed in the center of the screen. Slow. Irregular. Almost alive.

Behind you, the scientists whispered to each other. Their faces were pale, their eyes fixed on the red pulse. Then the lead researcher stepped forward.

He didn’t explain. He didn’t reassure. He didn’t even blink.

He just pointed at the screen and said:

“You. You must go.”

Alarms started — soft, urgent, like a heartbeat speeding up. A door slid open. A pack was shoved into your hands.

Inside: food rations, a coil of rope, a penknife, a metal rod, and a sealed envelope with your name on it.

The researcher grabbed your shoulder. “The island is waking up. Don’t let it finish.”

You were pushed to a small boat, engine already running. No crew. Just coordinates glowing on a cracked GPS screen.

Hours later, through the fog, an unmarked island emerged. A single house stood near the shore.

Inside: dust, silence, and a computer terminal with an irregularly flashing green light — like it had been waiting for you.

You pressed the power button. The screen flickered.

SYSTEM BOOT
